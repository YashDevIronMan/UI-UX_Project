Admin / Faculty Information & Availability

1. The Broken Experience

Students sometimes visit the admin or faculty office without knowing whether the person they need to meet is actually available. They may have to search for the person, ask other staff members, wait outside the office, or try calling and emailing them. If the person is busy, in a meeting, or away from the office, the student may have to wait for a long time or return another day. This can lead to unnecessary waiting, repeated visits, difficulty planning around classes, and wasted time, especially when the student has an important issue that needs to be resolved.

8. User Stories

1. As a student, I want to know whether the faculty/admin person is available before I go to the office, so that I don't waste time waiting.

9. Card Sort

I used 15 feature cards and asked people to group them based on what they felt belonged together. I did not give them fixed categories, so they could make their own groups. After comparing the results, I looked for the categories and groupings that appeared most often. The most common groups were related to Availability, Schedule & Timing, Contact & Meeting, and Office Information.. I used these common groupings to create my first Information Architecture. The card sort also helped me see which features students naturally connected with each other.

10. Information Architecture

After the card sort, I compared how the participants grouped the features. The main groups were Availability, Schedule & Timing, Contact & Meeting, and Office Information. I used these groups to create the first version of my Information Architecture.

11.Tree Test

After creating the Information Architecture from the card sort, I check whether students could find features under the correct categories.

Problems Found

  • Availability and Schedule were confusing for some users. Both categories were related to the faculty member's timing, so users were not always sure where to look for information about when the person would be available.
  • Meeting Request was not clear to everyone. Some users felt it belonged under Availability & Schedule because they would use it when trying to meet someone. Others felt it belonged under Contact & Meetings because it is a way of contacting the faculty member.
  • Office Location was easier to understand. Users were able to understand what this option meant because it directly tells them where the office is.
  • The test showed that some features needed to be grouped according to what the student is trying to do, rather than only by the feature name.

What I Changed

Based on the problems found during the Tree Test, I changed the Information Architecture to make the categories easier to understand.

  • Availability & Schedule — Availability Status, Busy Status, On Leave, Next Available Time, Office Timings, Today's Schedule, Weekly Schedule, Best Time to Visit, Waiting Time and Schedule Change.
  • Contact & Meetings — Contact Number, Email and Meeting Request.
  • Office Information — Office Location, Announcements and Working From Another Location.

The main change was to keep information about availability and timing together, while keeping actions related to contacting or requesting a meeting under Contact & Meetings. This makes it easier for students to decide where to look before visiting the office.

MVP Focus

The first version mainly helps students answer three things:

  • Is the person available?
  • When can I meet them?
  • Is it worth going to the office right now?

This keeps the first version focused on the main problem I found during my interviews and observation: students wasting time going to an office and waiting because they don't know the person's availability or schedule.
